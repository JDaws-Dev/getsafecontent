If you homeschool, you've probably pieced together a media strategy from five different tools that don't talk to each other.

One app for music. A browser extension for YouTube. Maybe a book list from your co-op. A prayer that Google SafeSearch actually catches the worst stuff. And a constant low-grade anxiety that something is going to slip through while you're teaching fractions to the younger kids.

I know because that was my house. My wife and I homeschool, and the media problem was always the thing we couldn't quite solve. Not because we didn't care, but because no single tool covered enough ground.

That's the gap SafeFamily was built to fill.

## The Problem With Piecemeal Solutions

Most families end up with a stack that looks something like this:

- **Music**: Apple Music with the clean filter on (which still lets through plenty of songs you'd never approve)
- **YouTube**: YouTube Kids for the little ones, regular YouTube with restrictions for the older ones (which barely works)
- **Books**: Trust the library, hope the cover art gives you a clue, maybe check Common Sense Media if you remember
- **Search**: Google with SafeSearch on (which only filters explicit images, not inappropriate text content)

Each one of these tools has gaps. And none of them give you a unified view of what your kids are actually consuming across all media types.

For homeschool families especially, this matters. Your kids use media as part of their education. Music for enrichment. YouTube for science experiments and history documentaries. Books for literature and research. Search engines for projects. It's woven into the school day, not just entertainment time.

You need tools that match that reality.

## One Family Code, Four Apps

SafeFamily uses a single family code across all four apps. Your kids log in once, and they have access to their approved content in SafeTunes, SafeTube, SafeReads, and SafeStudy.

Here's what that looks like during a typical homeschool day:

**Morning — Literature block.** Your 10-year-old opens SafeReads and picks up where she left off in *The Secret Garden*. The built-in reader tracks her reading streaks and progress. She wants to start a new book after this one, so she browses the genre library and requests *Anne of Green Gables*. You get a notification, check the AI content analysis, and approve it in 10 seconds.

**Mid-morning — Science.** Your 12-year-old needs to research the water cycle. He opens SafeStudy and searches. The AI filters results, gives him age-appropriate explanations, and logs everything so you can review later. No rabbit holes. No stumbling into something unrelated.

**Lunch break — Music.** The kids put on their SafeTunes playlists. Only songs you've approved. Your daughter discovered a new artist last week through the Discover feature and requested three songs. You reviewed the AI lyric analysis, approved two, and declined one. She wasn't upset — she's used to the system.

**Afternoon — History documentary.** Your son wants to watch a video about ancient Rome. He opens SafeTube and finds it in his approved channels. No autoplay. No algorithm. Just the specific channels you've vetted for your family.

That's four different media types, four different moments in the school day, all managed through one system with one family code and one parent dashboard.
